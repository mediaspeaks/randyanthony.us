Straight answers

Why do I have to pay up front?

That's the whole model. Buying 3, 6 or 12 months at once is how Mint cuts the price — no monthly billing overhead, no stores, no salespeople. In exchange you get rates the big carriers don't offer, and no phone bill until the term ends.

What happens when my $15/mo first term ends?

You renew onto a regular 3, 6 or 12-month plan. The 12-month renewal rates are $15/mo (6GB), $20/mo (17GB), $25/mo (23GB) and $30/mo (Unlimited) — renewing for a full year keeps the lowest rate. Shorter renewals cost more per month: on 3-month renewals those plans run $25, $35, $45 and $40/mo.

Which network does Mint use?

Mint runs on the T-Mobile network — the nation's largest 5G network, covering 98% of Americans with 5G and 99% with 4G LTE. If T-Mobile works where you live and travel, Mint will too. Check your specific coverage and phone compatibility on Mint's site before ordering.

Can I keep my phone and my number?

Yes to both, in most cases. Bring any unlocked, compatible phone and port your existing number over during activation. If your phone supports eSIM you can be up and running in minutes; otherwise Mint ships a physical SIM free.

Is "Unlimited" really unlimited?

There's no hard cap and you're never charged overages. The first 50GB each month is at premium 5G/4G speeds; past 50GB, speeds may slow for the rest of that month in busy areas. Hotspot is capped at 20GB/mo and video streams in SD (about 480p).

What if I pick the wrong amount of data?

You can upgrade to a bigger plan at any time by paying the difference, add 1GB for $10 or 3GB for $20 to get through a heavy month, or step down to a smaller plan when you renew. Mint shows your usage in the app, so by renewal time you'll know exactly what you actually use.
